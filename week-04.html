Week 04 · Thursday, September 24 · Phase 1 — Research & Proposal

UX Research & Synthesis

Target audience, pain points, competitive benchmarking, market position.

You have findings. This week they become a point of view: who this is for, what they're actually trying to do, and where the brand stands against everyone else in the category.

Due this week

Brand profile: audience, mission, archetype

  • Primary and secondary audience, evidence-backed
  • Mission statement in one sentence
  • Archetype with rationale
  • Competitive benchmark of three brands
  • 3–5 named pain-point themes

Session plan

01From findings to themes
Cluster your audit pain points. Three to five themes, each one named in the user's language.
02Target audience
Build one primary and one secondary audience from evidence — behaviors, context of use, motivations, blockers. No invented stock personas.
03Competitive benchmarking
Three competitors, same two page types. What they do better, and what nobody in the category has solved.
04Brand profile
Mission in one sentence. Archetype — pick one of the twelve and defend it against the closest alternative.
05Synthesis working session
Write the two paragraphs that connect audience → pain → opportunity.

In-class activity: Themes, audience, archetype

  1. Cluster audit findings into named themes. A theme with one piece of evidence is not a theme.
  2. Draft the primary audience: who they are, what they're doing when they hit this site, what stops them.
  3. Benchmark three competitors on the same two page types — screenshots side by side.
  4. Name the brand archetype and write the sentence that justifies it. Then write the sentence for the archetype you rejected.

Working with AI this week

AI is fast at category context and slow to be right about people. Use it for benchmarking scaffolds and audience hypotheses you then verify.

Prompts to start from

  • “Given this audit and these three competitors, what audience needs is the category as a whole failing to serve?”
  • “Here's my audience description. Where am I stating a preference as if it were evidence?”

What to verify

  • Audience claims need a source: reviews, forums, the brand's own positioning, or published category research.
  • Reject AI personas with names, ages and stock-photo lives. You want behavior, not biography.

Evidence to log

These feed the cognitive-process rubric. Capture them as you work.

Critical CurationWritten critique of an AI-generated audience profile and what you replaced
Reflective ReasoningWhy this archetype and not the nearest alternative
