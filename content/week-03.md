An audit is evidence, not opinion. Section by section, you name what's there, what's missing, and what it costs the user — with a screenshot attached to every claim.

## Due this week

### Live site audit

- Homepage and PDP, desktop and mobile
- Annotated screenshots for every finding
- Pain points written from the user's perspective
- At least three accessibility issues named specifically

## Session plan

01. **Audit methodology** — How to break a page into sections and interrogate each one: purpose, content, hierarchy, interaction, accessibility.
02. **Heuristics that matter in e-commerce** — Product findability, image quality and zoom, variant selection, price clarity, shipping and returns visibility, add-to-cart feedback, trust signals.
03. **Hands-on audit — desktop** — Homepage then PDP. Screenshot, annotate, log. Nothing gets written that isn't visible in a capture.
04. **Hands-on audit — mobile** — Same two pages on a phone. Note what breaks, what's buried, and what's simply been cut.
05. **Findings round-robin** — Two findings each, out loud. Vague findings get sharpened by the room.

## In-class activity: Section-by-section teardown

1. Capture the homepage and PDP at both breakpoints. Full-page screenshots, not viewport crops.
2. Slice each page into named sections (hero, category grid, gallery, buy box, specs, cross-sell, footer).
3. For each section, log: what it does · what's missing · the pain point · the opportunity.
4. Run a contrast check on body text, prices and buttons. WCAG failures are the easiest points on the rubric to earn and the easiest to leave on the table.

## Working with AI this week

- “Here's my section-by-section audit of a PDP. Which standard e-commerce PDP elements have I not mentioned at all?”
- “Rewrite this pain point so it names the user cost, not just the visual flaw.”
- Never let AI describe a page it hasn't been shown. If a finding isn't in your screenshots, it isn't a finding.

## Evidence to log

**Domain Knowledge Application**. Annotated audit with contrast checks and named anti-patterns
**Critical Curation**. Where AI's suggested findings didn't survive contact with the actual site
