Week 02 · Thursday, September 10 · Phase 1 — Research & Proposal

Brand Candidate Research

Using AI-assisted research to identify and compare three potential e-commerce brands for the class project.

Choosing well is half the grade. A brand with a rich PDP, real mobile problems and a strong visual world gives you something to design; a brand that's already well designed gives you nowhere to go.

Due this week

Brand comparative landscape (draft)

  • Three candidates evaluated against all six criteria
  • One-line justification per score
  • A recommendation with rationale
  • A note on what the two rejected candidates would have offered

Session plan ≈170 min

20 minShortlist share-out
Five brands each, fast. Class pushes back on the weak ones.
35 minThe six selection criteria
Visual potential, before/after impact, UI teaching value, PDP richness, mobile-first urgency, existing references — what each one actually means and how to score it 1–5.
45 minBuild the scoring matrix
Three candidates × six criteria, in Figma or a sheet. Scores get a one-line justification each — a bare number isn't an argument.
40 minAI-assisted market research
Use AI to gather category context, competitor sets and audience signals for your three. Log the prompts.
30 minVerification round
Open all three live sites on desktop and phone. Correct the matrix where reality disagrees with the research.

In-class activity: Three candidates, scored and defended

  1. Narrow five to three. Score each against the six criteria, 1–5.
  2. Write one line of justification per cell — 44 words of reasoning beats a spreadsheet of numbers.
  3. Name your recommendation and, more importantly, name what you're giving up by not picking the other two.
  4. Flag every claim that came from AI and hasn't been verified yet.

Working with AI this week

AI accelerates data-gathering. It does not get a vote in the decision.

Prompts to start from

  • “For [brand]: who is the actual customer, what's the price positioning, and who are its three closest competitors? Cite where each answer comes from.”
  • “Argue against redesigning [brand]. What would make it a weak choice for a student project?”

What to verify

  • Pricing, product range and competitor claims — check the live site.
  • Audience claims — check the brand's own marketing, reviews, or press before repeating them.

Evidence to log

These feed the cognitive-process rubric. Capture them as you work.

Critical CurationRevision trail showing where you corrected AI research against the live sites
Prompt ArchitectureLogged prompts for the market research round
