Week 10 · Thursday, November 5 · Phase 2 — Design & Presentation

Essential Design System, Part 1

Color palette — primary, secondary, neutral, semantic — and typography: typefaces, scale, line-height.

Your chosen direction becomes a system. Color and type first, because every component decision downstream inherits from them.

Due this week

Nothing submitted

A working week. Weekly assignment and participation still count.

Session plan ≈170 min

35 minColor architecture
Primary, secondary, neutral ramp, semantic (success, warning, error, info). Why neutrals should carry a hue bias, not be pure grey.
30 minContrast and accessibility
Build the palette to pass WCAG AA from the start: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI boundaries.
40 minType selection and pairing
Display and body faces that carry the direction. Licensing, weights available, and how the pairing behaves at 14px.
35 minType scale and line-height
A scale you commit to. Line-height by role, measure near 65 characters for running text.
30 minBuild
Everything defined as Figma variables and text styles, not one-off values.

In-class activity: Palette and type, as variables

  1. Define the full palette as color variables with real names — role names, not hue names.
  2. Contrast-check every text and UI pairing you intend to ship. Record the ratios.
  3. Set a type scale and build text styles for every role: display, H1–H4, body, small, caption, label.
  4. Test the pairing on a real sentence from your brand at every size.

Working with AI this week

Useful for contrast math and type-pairing candidates. Never for taste.

Prompts to start from

  • “Check these hex pairs for WCAG AA at body and large sizes; list the failures with the actual ratios.”
  • “Suggest six body faces that pair with [display face] and are available for licensing.”

What to verify

  • Recompute contrast in a real checker — AI arithmetic on ratios is not reliable.
  • Confirm every typeface's license before you commit to it.

Evidence to log

These feed the cognitive-process rubric. Capture them as you work.
